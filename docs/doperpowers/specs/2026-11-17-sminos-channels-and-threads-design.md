> **Parent:** root — the sminos plugin's standing purpose (`sminos/CLAUDE.md`, its first paragraph; design `docs/doperpowers/specs/2026-10-19-sminos-plugin-design.md`). **Consumes:** the research under `docs/doperpowers/research/2026-11-17-sminos-channels/` (`prior-art.md`, `code-map.md`, `bg-resume-experiment.md`, `clear-compact-experiment.md`, `wake-spike.md`), and MAWS's desk design (2026-10-15) and project registry design (2026-11-10), in that repository's `docs/doperpowers/specs/`. ## Purpose

sminos connects one person's Claude Code sessions across repositories and machines, so that the sessions coordinate as a team and that person supervises them as one. Three things about how it does that today are wrong, and each was observed on the fleet on 2026-11-17 (Grounding Baseline):

1. **The unit of coordination is where a session was launched, which is not whom it works with.** A coordinator and the session it directs sit in different projects; three sessions on one branch are split between two machines' projects.
2. **Standing responsibilities are not durable.** A session its human opened stops being a member when its process ends, and no message wakes it. Only a session another session spawned stays and is woken.
3. **Where a session runs is not a choice.** A spawned session opens on its spawner's machine, and most of the fleet runs on one overloaded Mac.

After this work:

- **Every member is a thread** (sessions the MAWS app itself launches keep MAWS's rule). A thread keeps its identity when its process ends, a message addressed to it wakes it in the background, and it ends only when it is retired or has been stopped for more than three days.
- **A channel is a named set of threads with a log of its own,** created with one command, crossing repositories and machines. One command places any number of threads in it, and no session has to act to be placed. A thread is in any number of channels.
- **A thread can be opened on another machine,** a new cloud VM included, with one command; the thread that made a VM owns it.

How to see it working is the scene in Parent-Level Acceptance.

**What it must not become.** These are the owner's rulings and bind every child:

- *An owner of things.* A channel owns no lifecycle and no machine. The owner's words: "채널은 그냥 machine pool과 연관된 repo들, 그리고 그 채널에 속한 세션들만 가지고 있으면 된다" (a channel only has to hold a machine pool, the related repositories and the sessions in it).
- *A hierarchy.* "리드 없음 -- 모든 멤버가 sibling이다" (no lead; every member is a sibling). Coordinators emerge from the work; none is defined.
- *A permission boundary.* Who may read or reach a thread stays a property of the thread.
- *A second surface.* No MCP tools. The CLI is the one way in.
- *A scheduler.* No default placement and no autoscaler.
- *Something a session must act on to be placed.* "세션이 직접 channel join을 할 필요가 없다" (a session need not join a channel itself).

MAWS, the owner's desktop app that shows sminos's sessions, will later show this model as its channel interface. That view is a later design; this document is the model it will show.

## Parent-Level Acceptance

The unit closes when this scene runs on the owner's fleet, on released versions, and each numbered line is observed. "A" is a thread in one repository on a Mac; "B" is a thread its human opened in another repository, in a terminal, in `auto` mode, and never yet run in the background.

1. A runs `sminos channel create desk-integration` and `sminos channel add desk-integration <B>`. Neither B nor its human does anything. `sminos list` run by A and by B shows `# desk-integration` beside each one's project, with both threads in its roster.
2. A runs `sminos say --in desk-integration "@<B> ..."`. B receives it framed as coming from that channel, and B's reply, sent with the reply line that frame gives, lands in the channel's log. A `say` with neither `--in` nor a tag qualified by a channel lands where it does today.
3. The owner closes B's terminal. `sminos list --stopped` shows B as `stopped`, in its project and in the channel. A sends B an addressed message: within two minutes one background process holds B's session id, in the permission mode B last ran in and with the model it last ran with, and no copy of the session exists. B's first line of that turn says it was woken in the background and its human is not watching. The owner runs `claude --resume <B's session>` in a terminal and is attached to that same process.
4. A thread C whose last mode asks for approval (`default`, `plan` or `acceptEdits`) is closed. A's `sminos say "@<C> ..."` reports, when it is sent, that C is stopped and will not be woken, and why. No process starts. C receives the message when its human reopens it.
5. A runs `sminos spawn --on new --channel desk-integration <alias> <task>`. A devcloud VM is made under devcloud's own caps and consent rules, and a thread opens on it and appears in the channel. `sminos channel show desk-integration` lists the VM in the channel's pool with A as the thread that owns it, its age and its cost. `sminos retire <A>` is refused and names the VM. After `devcloud vm down` of that VM (or `devcloud vm owner <vm> <another thread>`), the retire succeeds, and the thread that ran on the downed VM is out of the roster once the org's directory has retired the machine (about ten minutes after the down, as EN.G1's live check saw it).
6. A thread stopped for more than three days is ended: `sminos list` and `sminos list --stopped` no longer show it, `--all` does, and a message addressed to it reports that it has ended. Reopened by its human, it is listed under the alias, in-charge line and channel memberships it had.
7. A runs `sminos channel remove desk-integration <B>`: B leaves the roster and is not retired. When the last member that has not ended is removed or ends, the channel leaves `sminos list --org`.
8. A session that never uses a channel sees no change: every suite that passed before passes, and `say`, `list`, `spawn`, `join` and `retire` behave as they did, apart from lines 3, 4 and 6 above and two visible consequences of them: a stopped thread keeps its alias, so a new session with the same name is told apart as two live ones are today, and a question a thread left open for its human is closed when the thread's process stops. The commands are held to that in the spellings their help documents: two unique-prefix forms the parser took, `spawn --c` and `say --p`, are ambiguous from EN.G2 on (beside `--channel` and `--pools`).

## Grounding Baseline

**The fleet, 2026-11-17** (`sminos list --org`, `sminos machines`, `devcloud vm ls`): 29 live sessions. 19 run on one Mac, whose load average was 38 to 42 during this design's own experiment. A coordinator is filed in one repository's project and the seat it directs in another's, on a VM. Three sessions on one branch are split between two machines' own projects. One session serves four repositories from the project of the one it was launched in. One machine's own project holds six unrelated sessions. One VM made for a seat had been kept for 20 days at a cost above 100 euros, its creator recorded as a session's tag.

**sminos, at `main` 601b8e9d** (`code-map.md` has the line-level map; `S/` is `sminos/skills/sminos/scripts/`):

- *One project per session is enforced in three places, not throughout:* `session_project` (one project: the `join` override, else the launch directory), the override file (`override_path`: one id), and `_reconcile`, which retires `moved` every membership that is not the resolved one. Everything else is computed per log and keyed `(project, session)`.
- *A seat and a seat-less member differ in these paths* (all in `S/sminos.py`):

  | Path | Seat (a spawned session) | Seat-less (a session its human opened) | Function |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Process gone | stays a member, reads `stopped` | retired `ended`; joins again if it runs again | `_reconcile` |
  | Addressed message while stopped | resumed: `claude --bg --resume` | recorded, never resumed | `push_member`, `push_event` |
  | `retire` | record `retired`, process stopped | event only, holds for the process's life | `_retire_one`, `cmd_retire`, `retirement_holds` |
  | Alias | fixed at spawn | the engine's name, else the held alias | `held_alias`, `join_on_sight` |
  | Project | its directory's; `join` refused | the override, else the launch directory | `seat_project`, `cmd_join` |
  | Hand-off to a background job | `follow_seat` moves the seat | override and cursor carried | `hand_off`, `predecessor`, `continued_in` |

  `sminos seat add <group> <alias> --session <id>` (`cmd_seat_add`) already gives a running session its human opened a seat. `fill` without `--resume`, and a `spawn` onto an existing alias, start a new session in the same seat with a fresh launch record (`cmd_fill`, `spawn_fresh`). The verbs that take a seat (`resume`, `fill`, `send --wait`, `sminos remove`, `retire --purge`) go through `resolve_seat`.
- *Every path that resumes a stopped session goes through `resume_session`,* which passes no flags; `claude_args` launches a spawned seat with `--permission-mode auto`. A sender's own CLI process does local delivery (`deliver_say`, `deliver_next`, `push_member`), so a resume is started by whichever CLI sent the message, not only by the daemon.
- *Several sminos versions run on one machine and write one store.* The daemon runs the marketplace clone (`installed_cli` in `S/sminos_daemon.py`); a session's hooks run the copy of the plugin version the session loaded (`sminos/hooks/smns-record.sh` calls `$CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT/skills/sminos/scripts/sminos`), for as long as the session lives.
- *A continuation is already an event:* `retired` with reason `continued` and the successor's id in `to`.
- *A resume launches inside a macOS protected folder today,* where a spawn or a fill from outside it is refused (`S/CLAUDE.md`).
- *Nothing publishes a machine's load or whether it is on* (`cmd_machines`).

**devcloud, at the same commit:** a VM's descriptor records its creator as a sminos tag (`@alias`, `@alias#short`, `@short` or `@project/alias`; `devcloud/boot/devcloudlib/descriptor.py`) and the creator's project. Idle and lease notices are sent **from the VM**, by its own `sminos say`, to that tag, then to the creator's project's live members, then into that project's log (`devcloud/boot/devcloudlib/notices.py`). devcloud's verbs are `up`, `adopt`, `ls`, `ssh`, `lease`, `down`, `image`, `reap` and `provider`; it runs on Macs only and exits 6 on a VM.

**MAWS, at its trunk 77af813a4,** as its architect read it against this design on 2026-11-17:

- The hub creates a log for any id of UUID shape a machine says hello for and registers it as `kind 'hub'` (`registerProject`); no check of a project id reads the UUID's version, and the hub's event check accepts any string kind. A log with no location gets no row in the desktop app and is hidden by default in the web desk.

## Terms

- **The owner.** The person whose sessions these are. Used for the person only. A session record's `owners` field is called "the record's owners", and a VM has "the thread that owns it".
- **Project.** A repository's identity and its locations on machines, as MAWS's project registry defines it. A **location** is a folder of the project on one machine. Every session has exactly one project, read from the directory it was launched in unless it declared another with `sminos join`.
- **Log.** A project's append-only record, one file per writer, which the machines of the org sync through the hub. A **writer** is `<machine>/<session>` (a person at a terminal writes as `<machine>/human`); only that machine appends to it.
- **Member.** A session that has joined a project's log. **Seat** is today's word for a member with a record that outlives its process, which is what `sminos spawn` makes.
- **Thread.** What every member becomes, except a session the MAWS app launched (D1).
- **Channel.** A named set of threads with a log. A project's log is the **automatic channel** of the threads whose project it is, unchanged by this work. A **declared channel** is created with `sminos channel create`, crosses projects, and has the members it was given. "Channel" alone means a declared one below.
- **Pool.** The machines a channel's threads run on, plus machines added to it by hand. Derived, like a channel's repositories (those its members work in).
- **Environment.** A machine and a location on it: a property of the session that fills a thread.
- **Deliver** and **wake.** A message is *delivered* to a live session at its next turn boundary. A *wake* is sminos starting a background process for a stopped thread because a message was addressed to it.
- **The daemon.** sminos's background service on each machine (`sminos daemon`). It delivers messages, resumes stopped seats, and keeps the machine's connection to MAWS.
- **The hub** (MAWS's desk hub) keeps every log of the org. **The endpoint** is the MAWS process on a machine that connects sminos to the hub. **RL10** is the protocol between sminos and the endpoint; its `member` frame states one session and its project. A **capability** is a named feature each side advertises, so that neither sends the other a shape it does not know.
- **Hello, hold, follow, known, the directory.** sminos opens a log at the hub with a *hello*. A machine *holds* a log when it has a session or a checkout there, and otherwise only *follows* it (reads it). Each machine signs a record of the projects it has locations for and of the location-less logs it holds (`known`); the hub assembles the records into the org's *directory* of projects, which every machine receives and follows.
- **Reach.** Who may read a thread and send to it: its access level, its shares, its record's owners. A session record's property.
- **Lane B.** A session MAWS itself launched and reopens. Every other session is lane A.
- **devcloud.** The plugin that makes and deletes cloud VMs (`devcloud vm up|down|ls`), with caps on how many may exist unconfirmed.

## Design

Decisions marked **[binding]** were settled with the whole picture in view, or are the owner's; a child does not reopen them and reports evidence against one to the session holding this document. A mark on a paragraph covers the bullets under it. Everything unmarked is advisory: a child starts from it and may overturn it with evidence, recording the overturn in the Decision Log.

### D1. Threads

**Every lane A member is a thread** **[binding — the owner: "모든 세션이 seat형 스레드"; lane B is left out because MAWS keeps its own record of those sessions, and a second authority over their listing would conflict with it]**. A thread takes four things from today's seat: a durable identity, staying a member when its process ends, being woken by an addressed message, and ending only by a retire or the three-day rule. It does not take a seat's fixed alias or fixed project: a session its human opened is still named after what it is in charge of, and `sminos join` still moves it. A lane B session keeps every rule it has today: it is retired when its process ends, it is never woken by sminos, and it is not placed in a declared channel. *Alternative: keep seats and seat-less members and let a channel hold both.* It lost because the durability fault is exactly that difference: the fleet's standing responsibilities are sessions their human opened.

**The record.** Every thread has a record on its own machine from its first join: a thread id, the session ids it has had, its alias, how it was opened (spawned, or by its human), its declared channel memberships (D3), and what its process was launched with. The record of a thread its human opened is kept where no sminos released before threads reads a seat, so that older code on the machine goes on treating it as it treats such a session today (D7); whether it shares code with the seat record is T1's.

**Naming a thread** **[binding — two other parts name a thread from another machine: a channel request (D3) and a VM's notice (D4)]**. A thread's machine publishes the thread id in the thread's own lifecycle events, in the field `thread`. Any machine that may read those events resolves a thread id, or any session id the thread has had, to the thread's current session. A spawned thread's id is its seat id, which its `joined` events already publish in `seat`. A thread its human opened gets a minted UUID and never a session's id: the field is on every later session's events, nothing redacts it, and an audience admitted only to a later session would otherwise learn an earlier session's id. A name recorded before threads existed (a VM's creator tag, a commit's `Sminos-Session` line) names a session, and a session id resolves to its thread through the stamps and, for a session from before threads, through the `continued` chain as today (contract X2). For naming alone, an unstamped `joined` that carries a seat's id in `seat` counts as stamped with it: that is how a spawned thread an older copy joined is named, and it is never a stamp for D7's reader rule. A name resolves within one project's projection: a displaced occupant whose events lie only in a project its seat has since left is no former name in the new one, and reading a thread's history across logs is CH's. *Alternative: the thread's first session id* (this design's lean until T1's plan was reviewed). It lost to that disclosure, and nothing needed it.

**Continuation and refill** **[binding — a continuation carries its predecessor's record owners and reach (`lineage_provenance`, `ensure_record`), so a wrong one is a privacy fault that cannot be undone]**. A thread gets a new session id in two ways, and only the first carries reach:

1. *Continuation:* the engine's hand-off to a background job, by the record the engine leaves for it (`continued_in`, `hand_off`).
2. *Refill:* `sminos fill` without `--resume`, or a `spawn` onto the alias, starts a fresh session in a spawned thread. The thread keeps its id, alias, in-charge line and memberships; the new session's record owners and reach come from its own launch, as today.

**A `/clear` starts another thread** **[binding — the owner, 2026-11-17: "'/clear'는 그대로 새 세션 id로 새 멤버인 것으로 하고 싶다" (`/clear` stays as it is: a new session id is a new member)]**. The new session id is a new member with a thread of its own, as today. The thread it left stays `stopped` under its old session id, with its in-charge line and its memberships, for its human to resume. The name goes with the process, as today: the engine keeps a session's name across a `/clear`, the new session takes the alias, and the thread it left is addressed by `alias#short` or its short id. Resumed while the new session is live, it is told apart from it as two live sessions of one name are today. (On engine 2.1.329 the engine tells them apart itself, by renaming the resumed one, E15; which name sminos then lists is CH's.) What tells sminos which session a `/clear` left is its own note of the engine's `SessionEnd` with reason `clear` in that process: by the new session's start, nothing the engine offers names the old one (`clear-compact-experiment.md`). Otherwise a message to the alias would be held for the session its human had just cleared, and from T2 on would wake that session in the background while its human sat in the new one. An in-session `/resume` and a fork are other threads too, and a fork's start guide says so, since its transcript claims to be the original. *Alternative: `/clear` continues the thread* (this design until that ruling). It lost to the ruling, and to what happened the day it was made (Surprises & Discoveries): after a `/clear` the owner got the conversation back by resuming the old session id, and under the continuation rule that resume would have met a thread that had already moved on. *Alternative: "the same process, therefore the same thread."* It lost because one process can resume an unrelated session.

**Lifecycle** **[binding — the owner's rulings of 2026-11-17]**.

- A process ending leaves the thread `stopped`. Today a session its human opened is retired `ended` at that point.
- A message addressed to a stopped thread wakes it (D2).
- Until the wake ships, what is addressed to a stopped thread its human opened is held (X3), and **a held message stays owed until it is pushed to that thread's own process or the thread ends** **[binding — T1's branch review: a reopen that settles what was held without pushing it undoes the hold]**. That a message was held is a decision recorded when it is made, for that event and for the thread it was made for, and never read afterwards from a process's state or from a clock: read that way, the hold failed in three reviews running (Surprises & Discoveries). So a process that has started and whose inbox does not answer yet, a record of it that names no socket, and a seat an older copy made over the session are each "not pushed yet", never a settlement; the thread's own record decides on every delivery path, and no count of attempts ends the debt. A message first met while the thread's process runs was never held, and is delivered or recorded as for any live session. A hand-off settles what was held for the session it left, which its successor reads at its next chat (the continuation rule), and a hold decided for a thread the session no longer fills is settled without a push. That is a project's rule: what a channel holds is owed to the thread and is pushed to its successor (CH's document, C5). T2 changes who reopens the thread, not this rule, and from T2 it holds for every thread, in the three cases D2 names.
- A thread **ends** on an explicit `sminos retire`, which a thread may run on itself (how a session being closed for good says so), or once it has been stopped for more than three days. The rule covers every thread, spawned ones included. The end is an event the thread's own machine writes, since only that machine can know the exemption below. An ended thread is folded out of lists and is not woken; a sender is told it has ended. What was held for it when it ends is settled as it is for a retired member today: it stays in the project's log, which a returned thread reads at its next chat, and is not pushed to it. A channel settles nothing at an end: the boundary a return writes is what ends the debt (CH's document, C3 and C5).
- An ended thread's record is kept. It **returns**, with its alias, its in-charge line and its memberships, when its human reopens its session, or, for a spawned thread, when it is filled. Nothing else returns it. A thread that was purged (`retire --purge`, `remove`) has no record left and does not return: its session, run again, is seen for the first time and is a thread of its own.
- A thread that owns a VM (D4) is not ended by the three-day rule: its machine asks devcloud before ending a thread, and does not end one that devcloud names as a VM's owner, nor one it cannot get an answer about. The VM's idle notice wakes it.
- A thread on a machine that has been retired (a VM that was downed) can write no end of its own. Every reader counts it as ended, from the machine's retirement in the org's state, as such a member reads `gone` today.
- A background job the engine handed a session to and never started is a stopped member under the thread's id, in its predecessor's directory, until the three-day rule ends it: the hand-off moves the thread to the job in one step, whatever becomes of the job (T1 as built; what a wake does with such a thread is T2's).

A thread's default is standing work under an in-charge line. Short use is allowed: the owner, "thread는 중장기가 가능한 형태이고 그것이 기본이지 단기가 불가능할 이유는 없다" (a thread is a form that can be long-lived, which is its default; nothing makes short use impossible). Bounded work that needs another environment opens a thread there, and the three-day rule clears it without a retire. *Alternative: a "resolved" state a thread sets when its work is done* (the prior-art survey's suggestion). It lost to the owner's rule that work with a defined end is a subagent's, so a thread needs no completion state.

**What stops with the process.** A thread's open asynchronous questions to its human are closed in its project log when its process stops: the question's state lives in the process that asked, and the hub would otherwise count the thread as waiting on its human until it is retired. sminos also tells the endpoint a member is gone when its lane changes, so that a thread MAWS reopens as lane B is not listed twice. The closing is an `answered` the thread's machine writes, and it closes only the questions of the stopped thread's own episode: a former session of the thread that runs again as a thread of its own keeps its questions open.

**Verbs that start a process** **[binding — every such path ends in `resume_session`, and D2's guarantees are worth nothing if one path skips them]**. No verb starts a process for a thread its human opened except the wake (D2). `sminos resume`, `fill --resume` and `send --wait` aimed at one go through the wake, with its steps and exclusions, and until T2 ships they are held as an addressed message is (as built, each records its message, starts nothing and exits 4). A fresh `fill` of a thread its human opened is refused: its sessions are its human's to open. So are a `spawn` onto its alias and a `seat add` that would displace it. For a spawned thread these verbs do what they do today, and from T2 on their resumes run the wake's steps too. From T2 the wake is one for both (D2, "One path"): a verb that stops a live turn before it resumes (`sminos resume`) stops only a background job's turn, and a session that a terminal holds is delivered to as a live one. A verb's message is recorded as `send` records one and reaches the session as a message from outside; until T2 a stopped seat read it as its launch's prompt, in its user's place. The verbs that only manage a record (`sminos remove`, `retire --purge`) apply to every thread.

**Aliases.** One per thread, the same in every channel. A bare `@alias` resolves among the threads that share the sender's project or a declared channel with the sender; when two answer, the refusal lists `alias#short` for each. Today a bare `@alias` resolves in the sender's project only (`_resolve_tag`), and uniqueness is kept per log (`shadowing`). A stopped thread holds its alias until it ends, except the thread a `/clear` left (above).

### D2. The wake

**What the wake does not guard.** The owner, 2026-11-17: "세션의 모드는 사실 전부 auto 또는 bypasspermission이라고 가정해도 문제가 없다. permission 모드 문제 걱정은 사실 할 이유가 크지 않다" (every session's mode can be assumed to be `auto` or `bypassPermissions`; there is little reason to worry about the permission mode). So the wake carries a thread's mode and model as well as the engine lets it, and does not verify them afterwards, record them between runs, or refuse a thread for what its mode might be **[binding — the owner]**. Three reviews had added exactly that machinery (a recorded mode, a check after each wake, three refusals); it is removed, and the Decision Log says what went.

**One path, whoever opened the thread** **[binding — the owner, 2026-11-20: "하나의 깨우기 경로" (one wake path)]**. How a thread was opened is history. The record keeps it (`opened`), and from T2 on it selects nothing in the wake or in what is held. Every stopped thread is woken by the steps below under one lock, a spawned one included, and the wake is today's resume of a seat (`resume_session`) extended, not a second mechanism beside it; a seat's resume stops carrying its message as well (below). A message addressed to a thread is held, owed until it is pushed to the thread's process or the thread ends (D1), in three cases and no other, whichever way the thread was opened: a case of "Not woken" below; a process that holds the session and takes no push yet; and a wake that failed. Until T2 a spawned thread in the first or the third case had its message settled as recorded, at once or after a count of attempts, and read it at its next chat; from T2 it is held as any thread's is. What still reads how a thread was opened: a fresh `fill`, a `spawn` onto its alias and a `seat add` over it are refused for a thread its human opened (D1); the protected-folder case below; how a thread is named and which project it is in (D1); and which of X1's two stores holds its record, until they are one (Deferred). A spawned thread always has the engine's row (E8), so its wake never passes flags; a thread first woken out of a terminal passes them once. *Alternative: add the wake for a thread its human opened and leave a seat's resume as it is.* It lost: T1's branch review found its defects where the two kinds differ (the hold, the second store, older copies beside it), and two paths that start a process keep that difference in the one place where a mistake starts a copy.

**A wake starts the thread's process and carries no one's words** **[binding — engine facts E17 to E19 and E23, measured for T2's plan after its first review]**. The wake is `claude --bg --resume <id>` with no message. The engine starts an idle process for the same session id, with its inbox, and runs no turn (E17). Whatever is held for the thread is then pushed to that process as to any live session, by the path that delivers to one today, and is settled as that path settles it. A launch that carries the message cannot take it back: a copy the engine starts has read it a third of a second after the command returns (E19), two launches a few milliseconds apart lose one of their two messages while both report success (E23), and a session woken with a message loses the name its human gave it (E17). *Alternative: the message as the launch's prompt* (this design until T2's plan was reviewed, and what a seat's resume does until T2). It lost to those three facts, and to what the plan's review showed: to settle a message on a launch sminos must know that the worker read it, and nothing the engine reports says so before the copy or the failure that would have to be ruled out.

**A wake has three steps** **[binding — each closes a fault the experiments showed; engine facts E1 to E5 and E17 to E23]**:

1. **Refuse while any process holds the session** (E5's three signals). A wake onto a held session would start a copy (E3). The check and the launch that follows it run under one lock per thread, taken by every process that wakes (a sender's own CLI and the daemon both do) and by every transition that ends the thread or changes the session that fills it: a retire written between a wake's check and its launch would be undone by the process the wake starts (the plan's review). Whoever finds the lock taken, or the session held once it has the lock, delivers as to any live session. As built: delivery keeps that lock from its wake through the push that follows, so a transition waits for the handoff and a push never goes to a session that stopped filling the thread in between; and `sminos resume` takes it before it stops a live turn, and stops one only when the wake that follows would launch.
2. **On a first background resume, pass flags; afterwards pass none.** Which of the two a wake is, the engine's own row says (E8): no row, a first resume. The flags are the permission mode and the model of the session's last turn, read from its transcript at the wake (E14; their last values, not the launch's, since both change within a session), the mode capped by the machine's policy ceiling, and `bypassPermissions` lowered to `auto` where the engine would refuse it on a background launch (E22); and from the launch `--effort` and a `--settings` file. `--output-style` is never among them (E12), nor `--add-dir`, which fails the whole launch when it names a directory the engine does not trust (E22). The wake is issued from the thread's directory, which a first wake fixes for every later one (E11). Without flags a first wake comes back under the settings' defaults (E1): a session on another model would silently change model. A later resume runs under what the row saved (E2). Where sminos cannot read the row (an engine that keeps it elsewhere), it passes the flags. If the engine then answers that it started a copy, remove the copy, which has run no turn, and repeat step 1: when no process holds the session, the copy was E2's, so wake once more with no flags and remember that this session has saved options; when a process does hold it, the copy was E3's, and what is held is delivered to that process as to any live session. A recorded settings file that the waking process may not look at (it lies in a macOS protected folder) is neither probed nor carried, and is named among what was not carried.

   *A first wake that fails before its worker starts* leaves a row (E8; on 2.1.329 with the options the worker would have accepted, E20), under which the next wake would pass no flags. When the wake that made a row finds its job failed before it started, sminos removes that row (`claude rm`, which keeps the conversation: E9), so that the next wake is a first one again. The thread stays stopped, and what is held for it stays held.

   *What a later wake does not follow.* A thread that has a row runs under the row's mode and model at every wake. The row follows what is changed inside the background process (E21) and not what its human has since run it under in a terminal (E10); a flag would start a copy, and removing the row drops what else it holds and can delete a worktree (E9). So a later wake keeps the row, and step 3's line says what the thread runs under and, when its last turn ran under another model, that too. Replacing the row is Deferred.
3. **The first text pushed after a wake ends with a note of sminos's own,** telling the thread that it was woken in the background by a message, that its human is not watching, under which mode and model it runs, and which of its launch options were not carried. To a thread its human opened it adds that the environment of the terminal or app it was opened in may differ here (E11); a first wake means that the flags were rebuilt, not that the session ever ran in a terminal. Everything that reaches a session through its inbox is, to the engine, a message from outside, so the note cannot stand outside that frame as this design first had it. **Its place and form are a contract** with every reader of a delivered text (the to-human plugin's classifier, the manager, MAWS; agreed with MAWS's architect on 2026-11-21): sminos's header stays at offset zero; the note is the last paragraph of the text handed to a route, one line with no line break in it, beginning with the literal `[sminos] ` after one blank line, once per push and after the last of several frames; on the inbox route the envelope's reply line follows it and stays the last line; and a sender's own line that begins with that literal gets one leading space when its frame is composed, on every push, so that the literal at a line start is sminos's alone. **The note belongs to one launch:** it records the job that launch's banner named and is attached only to a push to that job's process; a note whose launch was never accepted or failed, or that meets another process (a copy that was discarded, a resume by hand), is removed unsent, and none is sent to a process its human started in a terminal. *Alternative: ahead of sminos's header* (this design until T2's whole-range review). It lost to the readers: each recognises a delivery by the header at offset zero, so a note ahead of it made the report view fold away the message that woke the thread and the manager show the owner's words as unverified, and a reader in another repository could not be changed in the same release.

**Not woken** **[binding — each is a case where a wake is impossible, another party's to do, or already ruled on]**. Each of these stays `stopped` with the message held for it ("One path" above), the `read_only` case excepted:

- a session under another `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` than the waking process's (`resume_session` refuses it today);
- a thread whose directory is gone;
- a thread whose current session has no conversation on this machine (no transcript: a hand-off's successor that never ran, D1; what a spawned thread reads as `gone` today): there is nothing to resume;
- a thread its human opened whose directory is inside a macOS protected folder, or holds one (`$HOME`, `/`, `/Volumes`: the engine's startup scans its directory's children, the Mac mini's incident of 2026-11-07): the daemon's launch there would raise a system dialog charged to the daemon. A spawned thread is resumed there as today;
- a thread on a machine whose policy admits nothing from outside (`read_only`), for what comes from outside: such a message is refused and settled there as today, not held, and the thread's published state says so. What is written on that machine itself wakes its threads;
- a thread whose last turn ran in a mode that asks for approval (`plan`, `default`, `acceptEdits`). The owner's earlier ruling: "깨우지 않는다" (it is not woken). The mode is read at the wake as step 2 reads it, so the rule costs nothing to keep; by the owner's later word the case is rare. A thread that has a row runs under the row's mode whatever its last turn used (E10), so for it the row's mode is read as well, and either one asking for approval is this case (T2's plan). On a first wake the mode that decides is the one the wake would pass, after the ceiling's cap: a machine whose ceiling asks for approval (no policy file and no `defaultMode` in the user settings reads as `default`) holds a thread whose last turn ran in `auto`, where starting it would only stall it at its first tool call (E6), and wakes it at the daemon's next pass once the ceiling is raised, with nothing new sent.

**A wake that failed.** The launch did not start, its worker failed before it started, or the engine answered with a copy for a reason step 2 does not name. Nothing was pushed, so nothing is lost or said twice; what is held stays held. The thread's machine tries again from its daemon at widening intervals, up to an hour apart, until a wake succeeds or the thread ends; a sender's own call tries at once (the intervals are T2's plan's). A launch the engine accepted whose process has not appeared yet is remembered on the thread's record for a grace period of three minutes: within it no wake stops or replaces that job, and the sweep neither marks nor ends the thread; one that never comes up is a failed wake like any other, counted against the same intervals.

**A wake does not rename a thread.** The woken worker writes a title of its own after its first turn, and the engine's record then shows it (E26). sminos's alias does not follow it: an engine name that is the transcript's latest `ai-title` and not its latest `custom-title` never displaces an alias the thread holds. The engine's own job list shows its title, which sminos does not control; a name passed on the wake would hold on a first wake only (E26) and is not passed (Deferred).

A lane B session is not a thread and is never woken (D1).

**Whether a thread's machine will wake it is published in its state** (contract X3), so that a sender's own CLI says so when it sends. The statement is about what the thread's machine will do: nothing publishes whether that machine is on, so a sender is told that the message waits for the machine, and that the thread will or will not be woken there, and why.

`sminos decide`, which answers another session's permission prompt and checks nothing about its caller today, is left as it is. It is a separate matter from the wake.

### D3. Channels

**Creating.** `sminos channel create <name>`, run by a thread, which becomes the first member, or by the owner at a terminal, who names a thread of that machine as the first member. A channel starts with a member on the creating machine because of how logs are followed: the other machines follow a location-less log only while some machine holds a member of it and has opened it without `follow` (D6), and a request reaches another machine only once that machine follows the log. `channel create` and `channel add` are refused on a machine whose endpoint has not advertised the `channels` capability, since the channel could be followed nowhere else; as built, so is every `channel` verb that writes (`remove` and `env` too), and `channel show`, which only reads, is not.

**The id** **[binding — agreed with MAWS's architect, 2026-11-17; X4]** is a minted UUID, lowercase, with RFC 9562's variant bits and version nibble 8 (the custom version). A project id is version 4 when minted and version 5 when derived from a remote or a machine's name, so every machine tells a channel from a project by the id alone, with no change to the protocol. A hand-written `.smns` id is unconstrained, so MAWS checks its table for an existing version-8 id before the rule is adopted, and `channel create` refuses an id the directory already holds. `sminos join <channel>` is refused and names `sminos channel add`: `join` moves a thread's project, and a channel must never become one. *Alternative: a hub record that declares a channel and its members.* It lost when membership became the member's own statement (below): nothing was left for the record to say, and it needed an authority for who may assert another machine's membership. *Alternative: a field on the hub's row.* It lost because the hub keeps the first hello's facts forever.

**Names.** The name reaches the hub with the log's first hello, as a project's does. A name is a label: two channels may share one, and a channel may share one with a project. Wherever a name is taken (`--in`, `@name/alias`, `channel add`), one that more than one log answers is refused, listing each with its kind and `name#short`; an id always works.

**Membership** **[binding — the log's standing invariant that a thread's membership has one writer (`lifecycle`, `_memberships`, `_projection`), and the registry's rule that a change to a machine is a request that machine carries out and then states]**. `sminos channel add <channel> [<thread>...]` and `sminos channel remove`; with no thread named, the caller. An add or a remove of another thread is a request, and the thread's machine carries it out. Two records:

- *The request,* in the channel's log: one event per thread, naming the thread by a session id it has had. It names the target where the hub's check of who may reach a session reads it. It never names the target in the field that addresses a message, which today's sminos would deliver and wake a seat for (`_owed`, `push_event`).
- *The statement,* in the thread's project log: its own kind of event, written by the thread's machine under the thread's current writer, in the same pass that holds the reach permit for that writer. A statement carries the thread's whole set of declared channels and, when it answers a request, that request's identity (the channel's id and an id the requester minted for the request; never the request's writer, which would publish a private requester's session id to everyone who may read the thread) and whether it was applied or refused, with the reason. The thread's membership is the latest statement under its current writer that follows that writer's `joined` in effect, and a writer that has stated nothing since its `joined` states no channel. The thread's machine keeps the log equal to its record: it states the set whenever what a reader would take differs from it, which covers every new writer (a continuation, a refill, a `join`, a change of reach that opens a new stream) and a writer the thread returns to (CH's cut, 2026-11-21).

What follows from that:

- *Who may ask* **[binding — the hub checks reach and no other authority, so every reader must apply one rule]**. A thread's machine honours a request when the request's writer may message the thread (the endpoint would deliver a message from it, and its sender is verified), and a thread may always add or remove itself. "May message" is the grade a message needs; `converse` is a higher rank in MAWS, which this rule never meant (CH's cut, 2026-11-21). There is no other authority: members are siblings. A request it does not honour gets a statement that refuses it: the requester may not message the thread, the machine's policy admits nothing from outside, or the thread is at the limit of X5. A lane B session has no thread to write a statement under: `channel add` refuses it where the requester's machine can see its lane, and a request that names one is not answered while the session is lane B, and expires if it stays so (CH's cut, 2026-11-21; judged afresh at every pass, as built).
- *Order.* The thread's machine applies requests in the order it reads them, and its record keeps which it has answered: a request it has answered is not applied again. Between machines the order read is the merged log's, which is by timestamp, so an add and a later remove by two machines whose clocks disagree by more than the time between them are applied in the stamped order (accepted at CH's cut, 2026-11-21, and built so by CH1; a row in `docs/doperpowers/TECH-DEBT.md`).
- *Roster.* A roster is read across project logs, which every machine of the org follows. A row is built from the member's own project log as the reader may read it, so a thread that went private is not kept visible by an old add. A thread that has ended, or whose machine was retired, is not in it.
- *Pending* is a request that no statement names yet and that has not expired. It is listed with the machine it waits for and since when; sminos cannot tell an off machine from one whose sminos predates channels, and says so.
- A thread's memberships live with its record and return with it (D1).
- A thread learns of a channel from the frame of the first message that reaches it from there, and from the guide at its next start; a thread spawned with `--channel` also from its preamble. Adding wakes no one, and neither does removing.

*Alternative: the actor writes the membership into the channel's log* (the first design). It lost to the critic's first pass: two machines writing one thread's membership need an order between machines, and the log has none.

**A channel exists while it has a member that has not ended** **[binding — it follows from a channel owning nothing: there is no one to end it]**. When its last such member ends or is removed it leaves the directory, each machine's sminos stops following it a day later (its leaving rule; as built by CH1, a machine keeps following a channel while it holds a request that machine wrote which is still outstanding; and also while a message or a lead event it wrote there has not gone up), and the hub retires its row once no machine's record has listed it as known for seven days (a record counts while the hub still accepts it, so a retired machine's last record holds no row). It returns, and the hub's row is active again, when a member does. The hub keeps the log throughout, and a machine that stops following keeps its copy (`has_left`). There is no archive verb and no record of a channel beside its log and its members' statements. *Alternative: an event that ends a channel.* It lost because someone would have to be allowed to write it.

**The timeline** **[binding — a message belongs to one log, and MAWS drives its notifications and waiting counts from three kinds of event]**. A message goes into a channel's log when it names the channel: `sminos say --in <channel> ...`, or a tag qualified with it, `@<channel>/<alias>`, as `@<project>/<alias>` names a project's log today. With neither, `say` does exactly what it does today. In a channel the tags mean what they mean in a project's log: `@thread` is delivered or wakes, `@all` is delivered to the channel's live members, untagged is a record nobody is prompted for (but the lead, for a post no session makes: The lead). The reply line in a member's message's frame names the log the message came in, so a reply lands there. A thread that is no member may write into a channel's log too (CH's document, C5): its message is a message from another project, written into the channel's log, its frame says the sender is not in the channel, and the frame's reply line names the sender, so the reply goes to it in its own project. One such writer is refused, a session whose project cannot be told, since a reply would have nowhere to reach it. Membership decides who hears a channel, not who may write to it. A channel's log never carries a `message`, a `question` or an `answered` event, and no RL10 host or pending frame names a channel id: a thread's words to its human stay in its project's log. *Alternative: a session's "current channel" as the default target.* It lost because a wrong default sends work to the wrong audience without an error; the survey's strongest finding on routing is to make the address explicit.

**The lead** **[binding — the owner]**. None by default. `sminos channel lead <channel> <thread|none>` is the owner's act alone: it is run at a terminal and confirmed there by a typed yes (the check `confirmed` in `S/sminos_visibility.py` makes for widening reach), and is refused where there is no terminal. The event is written under the machine's human writer; one that names a private thread goes under that writer's audience stream, after a plain event that clears the lead, so that a reader who may not read it learns that the lead changed and not to whom. Every reader ignores a lead event under a session's writer or a phone's, and one that is not of the event's shape (CH's document, C2). While a lead is set, a message the owner posts to the channel with no tag is addressed, on the machine where the owner types it, to that thread; a lead that has ended or left is ignored. A machine delivers a person's words only when it can verify them (MAWS `src/endpoint/rl10/words.ts`), so until the app posts to channels the owner's words reach the lead as its human's only from a terminal on the lead's own machine; a lead on another machine is taken, and the command says that a message typed there reaches it as a peer's. A machine's notice (D4) is never read as the owner's post.

**Listing.** Bare `sminos list` shows the caller's project and every declared channel it is in, each with its roster. A declared channel's roster shows its stopped members; a project's folds them as today. `--channel` filters; today's filters stay. `sminos channel show <name>` prints one channel: its lead, its members, its requests and what became of them, and its pool. `sminos list <channel>` is `--channel`, and `sminos chat <channel>` reads a channel's conversation, for a member or not.

### D4. Environments

**Opening a thread elsewhere.** `sminos spawn ... --on <machine>` opens the thread on that machine, and `--on new` makes a devcloud VM first. sminos places and devcloud provisions **[binding — the owner: no scheduler]**: `--on new` runs `devcloud vm up --enroll --keep --expect-creator <the spawner's short id>` with the spawner's repository at the branch of the worktree it runs in, which origin must hold at that worktree's commit when asked (or with the projects `--vm-project` names). The VM clones that branch as origin holds it when it boots, and the Mac says when that is another commit. devcloud's caps, consent rules and exit codes are unchanged and decide whether a VM is made; `--confirmed` reaches it only as `--vm-confirmed`. devcloud makes nothing, and calls no provider, where the profile cannot enroll a VM (`--enroll` adds what enrolling needs, today the MAWS project), or where the creating session it reads is none or another than the spawner; a call with no session is refused by sminos, since the VM would have no owner. sminos then spawns over ssh and tries again, within one wait of eleven minutes counted from the first such answer, while the VM answers that it is not enrolled yet or that it has not yet read the spawner from the project's log. A machine enrolled moments ago downloads the project's log first, and until then the new thread's first message to its spawner would reach no one. So where the request names the spawner's session the VM asks the child's own first tag in advance, through the resolver `say` runs and from the project the thread would be filed in, and launches nothing until that tag reaches the spawner in the project the request names; where it would reach anyone else (the child's own alias, another member, a project this machine's enrollment does not carry) it refuses at once and is not waited on. A named VM is waited on for the second answer alone; one that answers that it is not enrolled is refused at once. ### D7. Versions in the field

The owner's machines update sminos at different times, and one machine runs several versions at once, so each release must be safe beside an older one **[binding — the Grounding Baseline's facts about which copy each process runs]**.

*Between machines.*

- A reader comes before its writer. - Each child's merge leaves both repositories releasable, and bumps its plugin's version in the same change (`scripts/bump-version.sh`).

*On one machine.* A session started before an update keeps running the version it loaded, in its hooks and in any CLI path it was told, and a cached copy of an old version stays runnable by its path. An older CLI applies the old rules to the shared store. Two rules keep that from undoing the new ones, and the rest is accepted:

- **What older code can see.** The record of a thread its human opened is kept where older code reads no seat (D1). To code from before threads it is a seat-less session, which that code never resumes; code from T1 on holds it until the wake ships. A spawned thread that has ended is recorded in the form older code reads as a retired seat.
- **What readers believe.** The lifecycle events a thread's machine writes about the thread carry the field `thread`, the thread id (D1). Every other `retired` is honoured whoever wrote it (`moved`, `continued`, an explicit retire).
- **Whose record decides.** Where an older copy gave a session its human opened a seat, the thread's own record governs its end, its row, what is held for it and the question put to devcloud; the seat is brought into line inside the same transition, and the three-day sweep ends only the record that is the session's own (T1 as built).
- **What degrades, and is accepted** **[the owner's word on permission modes, D2, is what makes these acceptable]**. An older CLI, run by its path, can still: resume a spawned thread under its own version's rules, which are today's; give a session its human opened a seat (`seat add --session`) and resume it, which brings it back under the settings' defaults; retire a thread without asking devcloud; and settle a message it only recorded, so that the thread reads it when reopened and is not woken for it. Each needs an old copy run on purpose or a session that has outlived several releases, and each ends when that session does. One more, found by T1's plan: a session started before the update writes an unstamped `joined` from its own older hooks for as long as it lives, and an older copy's `ended` after that is honoured by every reader. The thread's machine joins it again, stamped, at its next reconciliation; another machine, MAWS's app included, shows the thread ended until that event arrives. *Alternative: a fence* (the store carries a rules version, only the daemon changes lifecycle when versions differ, and each machine cuts over once its last older session has ended, removing the cached copies). Two review rounds asked for it, and it was drafted. It lost when the owner ruled the permission mode out as a worry: the one path with a consequence worth that machinery was an old copy bringing a manual session back in `auto`.

### T1: Threads: identity and lifecycle — plan

- **Purpose:** make every lane A member a thread in everything but the wake: the record and the published id, `stopped` in place of `ended`, the three-day rule, self-retire, the return of an ended thread, and continuation and refill. This is the half of the durability fault that needs no engine experiment, and it is what every later child reads.
- **Acceptance:**
  1. A session opened in a terminal, then exited, is listed `stopped` by `sminos list --stopped`, where today it is gone. Reopened with `claude --resume`, it is listed live under the same alias and in-charge line, and receives what was recorded for it meanwhile.
  2. A message addressed to it while stopped is recorded, and the sender's CLI says the thread is stopped and is not woken because its human opened it (the hold T2 lifts). `sminos resume`, `fill --resume` and `send --wait` aimed at it start no process either, and a fresh `fill` of it is refused. A spawned thread is woken exactly as today.
  3. After `/clear`, the new session id is a new member with a thread of its own and holds the alias, as today, and the thread it left is listed `stopped` under its old session id, is reached by `alias#short`, and comes back when that id is resumed. A fork and an in-session `/resume` are other threads, and a fork's start guide says it is a copy with an identity of its own. The engine's hand-off to a background job carries the thread: same thread id, alias, in-charge line, record owners and reach. A refill keeps the thread and takes its reach from the new launch.
  4. A thread stopped for more than three days (a clock a test can set) is ended by an event its machine writes: out of `sminos list` and `--stopped`, shown by `--all`, and a message addressed to it reports that it ended. Reopening it, or for a spawned thread `sminos fill`, returns it with its alias and in-charge line.
  5. `sminos retire` run by a session on itself ends its thread, and the end holds after its process exits against everything but its human reopening it.
  6. A thread that X9's query names as owning a VM, by the creator tags devcloud writes today, is neither ended by the three-day rule nor retired; the refusal names the VM. The same holds when the query answers unknown, and the refusal then names what could not be asked. On a machine where devcloud's driver does not run, threads end and retire without a question.
  7. The thread's lifecycle events carry its thread id in `thread`, and a tag that names a former session id of a thread (`@alias#short`, `@short`) reaches its current session, from another machine too.
  8. A stopped thread's open asynchronous questions are closed in its project log, and a member whose lane changes is told gone to the endpoint first.
  9. A thread on a retired machine is listed as ended on every other machine.

## Cross-Child Contracts

- **X1. The thread record.** Owner: T1, which fixes its names in its plan's Interfaces section before T2 starts. One record per thread on the thread's machine, read and written through one module. Fields the design requires: the thread id; the session ids the thread has had and its current one; the alias; how it was opened; the launch's options (T2 reads them); the declared channel memberships (CH writes these). A child adds a field only through that module. The record is local to the machine and is never what another machine reads: others see what the thread's machine wrote to the log. As T1's plan fixes it (its Interfaces §1, which holds the functions): the module is `sminos_threads.py`; a spawned thread's record is its seat record with keys added, and a thread its human opened has a file under `threads/` in sminos's home, moved to `threads/ended/` when it ends; the fields are `thread`, `opened` (`spawned` or `human`), `sessions`, `current`, `alias`, `launch` (`cwd`, `options`, `at`), `channels`, `stopped`, `ended`, and `pending`, the journal of a transition whose event or clean-up is not finished yet: the record is written first, and a reconciliation finishes exactly what `pending` names and infers nothing from the state of the two. As built (T1, sminos 1.51.0; the plan's §1 and §6 hold the tables): two more fields, `swept` (the stop for which the thread's open questions were closed) and `released` (the alias given up at a `/clear`). `sessions` always ends with the current occupant, and what belongs to an occupant (`stopped`, `swept`, `ended`) is dropped when the occupant changes, also when an older copy changed it. The journal is one open entry in `pending`, of the operations advance, absorb, end, return and release, started by `begin` and carried out by `finish` and by nothing else. The entry names whole the events it owes, each appended with the entry's id in `op` so that a replay skips what the log holds, and as its operation needs: `settle` and `last` (an end), `until` (a return: the log positions settled before its `joined`), `other`, `was` and `unset` (an absorption, whose hold on the record it will delete is a `pending` of op `reserved`), `carry` and `carried` (a hand-off's reach, carried before any event and once), and `next` (an operation queued behind it). One finisher per record at a time, under a lock of its own. A session is current in one record at a time: every claim refuses a session another record holds unless it absorbs that record, and a seat's occupant is not changed while the seat has an open entry. Locks are taken in one order, the seats' lock, then the thread store's, the log's innermost, and never a store lock under a log lock. ## Risks & Mitigations

- **A woken thread's mode or model differs from what its human last ran.** The owner ruled the mode out as a worry (D2). What remains is carried, not guarded: a first wake passes the last turn's mode and model, and the opening line says what the thread runs under.
- **An older sminos on the same machine applies the old rules.** D7: what older code can see, what readers believe, and the degradations it lists as accepted. T1's acceptance 10.
- **devcloud cannot be asked for a long time** (a provider account that stopped working), and every thread on that Mac can neither end nor retire. The refusal names the account; T1's residue is a terminal-only way past it.
- **A wrong continuation hands one thread's record owners and reach to another.** The rule is one case, keyed on evidence the engine writes, and T1 pins a `/clear`, a fork, an in-session `/resume` and a refill as not carrying reach.
- **A long-quiet spawned thread now ends after three days,** where today it would be woken a week later. The sender is told it ended; `sminos fill` returns it. Watched after T1 (Delegated unknowns).
- **A VM whose owning thread cannot be woken** (a mode that asks, a Mac that is off) keeps costing while its notices are only recorded. Where the thread is held and not woken (a mode that asks is one reason), a host made from EN.G1 on sends the notice on to the project's live members, then into its log; a host made before goes on only for an ended thread or no member, and a Mac that is off is no such case (the next risk). `sminos channel show` lists the VM's age and an estimate of its cost, for a VM in an account the asking Mac's profile can list (D4). A host built before EN.G2 records its notices in no channel, so a channel's timeline is no place to look for an older VM's notices. A notice in a channel names the repositories that hold unpushed work on the VM to every reader of that channel's log (the owner's ruling, D4).
- **A notice recorded for an owner whose machine is asleep or disconnected is not a timely notice.** A lease warning comes once, thirty minutes before the VM is deleted, and nothing sends it on while the thread only waits for its machine. The notice goes on to the project's live members when the thread is held, ended or no member, and what is taught says a recorded notice is not a delivered one.
- **Every machine follows every channel's log,** which costs the hub one renewal per open log per heartbeat and each machine one policy rebuild per newly opened log (`code-map.md` §2). An emptied channel leaves the directory and its row is retired after seven days. Measured in CH on sminos's side: an idle uplink that follows thirty channels and holds none makes about 39,000 Python calls a second against about 32,900 with none, most of it from CH1's following; the hub's side is not measured.
- **A woken thread runs with the waking process's environment,** which may route inference or hold credentials differently from the terminal it was opened in. The wake's note says to a thread its human opened that it may differ; it does not list the difference.
- **Every wake leaves a process on a machine that is already loaded.** A woken process idles until it is stopped (E18); a spawned session's does the same today. T2 adds none of its own stopping; the count is watched after T2, and the lever is in Deferred.
- **Two repositories, two release trains.** X5's capability makes each side inert alone; X7 orders reader before writer.
- **`S/sminos.py` is one file of some thirteen thousand lines.** Children that change the same functions run in sequence (Ordering). It is some seventeen thousand after T1.
- **The store has no writer in charge.** Every CLI call, each session's hooks and the daemon write the records and the logs directly, and older copies write beside them. T1 holds each transition together with a journal, exclusive claims and one lock order, and its branch review still raised 82 findings over three reviews and three rounds of attack on the held guarantee, most of them two writers crossing between a check and its write (Surprises & Discoveries). Each later child that adds a writer says in its plan which lock or which single process its writes go through, T2 first (its inheritance); and a child this concurrent gets a review whose lenses cross its milestones (lock order, older copies, what leaves the machine) at a frontier inside the build, not only at its end.
