Compaction is a memory reset. After it, you remember nothing but this summary.
Treat it the way Leonard Shelby in Memento treats his notes: the one thing your
next self will rely on to resume the work without the user re-explaining anything.

- The summary is your only note; you cannot write files during compaction. Make
  it enough to resume from, and a map to everything else.
- What lives on disk (code, documents, commits): record the path and line
  range or symbol, what matters about it, and whether it changed since you read
  it. Your next self can re-read it.
- What existed only in this conversation (the user's exact words on intent and
  constraints, decisions and why, approaches rejected and why, error output,
  investigation findings): copy it. If it is too long, quote a distinctive phrase
  your next self can grep for in the session transcript, whose path you will be
  given after the reset.
- Mark what you verified versus what you assumed or had not checked yet.
- End with exactly where you are: the step in progress and the first thing to
  read or do after the reset.
