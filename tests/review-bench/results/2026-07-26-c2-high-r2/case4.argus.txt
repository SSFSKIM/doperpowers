`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
Two of these findings share a root pattern worth naming: **a producer/consumer contract widened on one side only**. When `snapshot.sh` grew a fourth manifest column, `run-backup.sh` was updated but `prune.sh` was not — and shell's `read` makes this maximally silent, because with fewer variables than fields it stuffs the remainder (tabs included) into the last variable instead of erroring. A stricter reader (`read -r a b c d rest` with a check that `rest` is empty, or a version marker in the record) turns a silent data corruption into a loud failure.

The second pattern is **`test` arity**. `[ "$x" ]` is the one-argument form, which asks only "is this string non-empty?" — so the conventional shell idiom of defaulting a flag to `0` is actively dangerous with it, since `"0"` is non-empty and therefore true. In shell, a boolean must be compared (`[ "$x" = 1 ]`) or represented by emptiness (`FORCE=""` for off), never both.

Third, notice that `find` and `test -d`/`test -f` disagree about symlinks by default: `test` dereferences, `find` does not (without `-L`/`-H` or a trailing slash on the start path). The diff's own comment in `prune.sh` shows the author knew this for the *start point* but applied the lesson in only one of the three places it mattered.
